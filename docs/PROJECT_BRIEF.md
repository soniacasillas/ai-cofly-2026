# Project brief

## Event

**Jornadas AI Co-FLY 2026**

The event is addressed mainly to groups of high-school teachers and students, plus other interested participants.

Talk slot:

> **11.00–11.30 — “Cómo la inteligencia artificial está aprendiendo el lenguaje de la vida” — Sònia Casillas (Universitat Autònoma de Barcelona, UAB)**

The spoken talk itself should last approximately **15–20 minutes**, with a practical target of **17–18 minutes**.

## Original abstract

> Un recorrido por algunos de los avances más sorprendentes de la IA en las biociencias, desde la predicción de estructuras de proteínas y la interpretación del genoma hasta el descubrimiento de nuevos fármacos y la conservación de la biodiversidad. Un viaje desde las moléculas hasta los ecosistemas para descubrir cómo estas herramientas están ampliando nuestra capacidad para comprender y proteger la vida.

## Audience

Primary:
- high-school students,
- high-school teachers.

Secondary:
- scientists or university attendees outside AI/biomedicine,
- other interested members of the public.

Assume:
- basic familiarity with DNA, genes, proteins and cells,
- little or no formal machine-learning knowledge,
- heterogeneous scientific confidence.

## Communication goal

The audience should not merely hear that “AI can do many things”.

They should understand **why AI is particularly powerful in modern biosciences**.

The key reason:

> Biology now produces enormous data spaces and possibility spaces. AI can help identify patterns and prioritise the small subset worth deeper experimental study.

## Main conceptual framework

### Five verbs

1. **RECONOCER**
2. **PREDECIR**
3. **BUSCAR**
4. **DISEÑAR**
5. **MONITORIZAR**

These should be introduced early and revisited at the end.

### Two transversal ideas

#### 1. Scale / search-space idea

AI does not only make existing tasks faster.

It lets researchers explore spaces that are otherwise practically intractable:
- huge protein universes,
- billions of possible DNA substitutions,
- millions of chemical compounds,
- millions of images,
- thousands of hours of environmental recordings.

The memorable line is:

> **No podemos probarlo todo. La IA nos ayuda a decidir dónde mirar primero.**

#### 2. Validation idea

AI outputs are hypotheses, scores, candidates or predictions — not automatic truths.

The scientific cycle should be explicit:

> **DATOS → IA → PREDICCIÓN / PRIORIZACIÓN → EXPERIMENTO / OBSERVACIÓN → NUEVOS DATOS**

Memorable line:

> **Una predicción no es una verdad.**

## Tone

- curious,
- rigorous,
- optimistic without hype,
- accessible without being patronising,
- visually sophisticated,
- scientifically grounded.

Avoid framing AI as magic or as an autonomous scientist.

## What NOT to do

- Do not teach neural-network architecture.
- Do not spend several slides on ethics/bias; the broader programme already covers these.
- Do not overload with many extra tools/names.
- Do not use generic “AI brain” imagery.
- Do not make biodiversity monitoring the entire second half; other sessions at the event cover related topics in depth.
- Do not imply AI replaces doctors, structural biologists, genomicists, chemists or ecologists.

## Journey through biological scales

The talk should feel like zooming out:

**protein → tissue/image → genome → chemical space → ecosystem**

The examples:
- AlphaFold / AlphaFold 3,
- RFdiffusion,
- Virchow,
- AlphaGenome / AlphaGenome Atlas,
- halicin,
- BirdNET.

## Potential audience questions to be ready for

- Does AlphaFold make experiments unnecessary?
- Can AlphaGenome tell whether a mutation causes disease?
- Can AI invent life?
- Can an AI design a dangerous organism?
- How does a model “learn” DNA?
- Does AI see a tumour the same way a doctor does?
- Was halicin invented by AI?
- Can BirdNET identify every bird correctly?
- Where do the training data come from?
- Can AI be biased?
- What happens when the model sees something unlike its training data?
- Are these tools open / can students use them?
- Will AI replace biologists?
- Is ChatGPT the same kind of AI as AlphaFold?
- What is a “foundation model”?
